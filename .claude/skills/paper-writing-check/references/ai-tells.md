# AI tells to remove from paper text

Rewrite every match. A pattern is only acceptable when it is the plainest way to say the thing.

## Words and phrases

- Formulaic transitions: notably, importantly, crucially, moreover, furthermore, additionally,
  indeed, in addition, it is worth noting, interestingly.
- Inflated verbs: leverage, utilise, underscore, highlight (as "this highlights"), delve,
  showcase, foster, navigate, unlock, harness, elucidate.
- Vague or wrong-register verbs in results: "survived" (say "remained significant after",
  "passed"), "held up" in formal text, "landscape", "realm", "tapestry".
- Empty intensifiers: very, truly, significantly (unless statistical), clearly, robust (unless
  a specific test was run), comprehensive, novel (let the reader judge).
- Stacked hedges: "may potentially", "could possibly suggest", "appears to perhaps".

## Structures

- Default lists of three (three adjectives, three clauses) where two or four is the truth.
- "Not X, but Y" or "X, not Y" used for drama rather than to correct a real misreading.
- Colon chains and set-ups: "The answer: ...", "Two things matter: X and Y", a colon in
  every other sentence.
- Stated takeaways at the end of a paragraph ("This highlights the importance of ...",
  "Taken together, these results underscore ...").
- Manufactured closure or a flourish as the last sentence of a section.
- Atmospheric openers ("In recent years, ...", "In the rapidly evolving field of ...").
- Em-dash pile-ups and parenthetical asides in every sentence.
- Rhetorical questions in the body text.
- Bullet lists with bold lead-ins inside prose sections.
- Paragraphs where every sentence has the same length and shape.

## Discourse-level tells

- The text summarises instead of saying what was done and found.
- Implicit statements: the sentence relies on a term or result the reader has not met yet.
- Single-track structure: claim after claim with no example, no concrete case, no number with
  its meaning.
- "Abstract noun" style ("the assessment of the evaluation of explanations") where a verb with
  a subject would do ("we tested whether the explanations ...").
